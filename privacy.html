Privacy Policy

Home.io · last updated November 2026

In short: Home.io collects no personal data. Your home, your floor plans and your accessories stay on your devices and in your own iCloud account. We never see them.

Your home and accessories

Home.io shows the accessories in Apple Home (HomeKit) and, if you connect it, your Home Assistant server. It reads their names, rooms and states and sends your commands to them directly from your device. None of this is sent to us. A Home Assistant address and access token you enter are kept on that device only (the token in its keychain).

UniFi cameras and doors

If you connect a UniFi console, Home.io talks to it directly at the address you enter, normally on your home network. It reads your Protect cameras (their names, whether they're online, doorbell rings, and person, vehicle, animal and package detections) and your Access doors (whether each is locked and whether it's open). It shows the cameras' still pictures, and sends an unlock only after your PIN, Face ID or Touch ID. Pictures, rings, detections and unlocks go only between your device and your console, never through us or the home hub.

The API key and token you enter are kept only in that device's keychain: they are never synced to your other devices or put in Home.io's backups. The console's address, the security certificate you confirm and the list of your cameras and doors stay on that device too, and aren't in Home.io's backups, though your device's own iCloud or computer backup can include the address and the list. If you put a UniFi camera or door on your floor plan, hide it or change its icon, its name and room go with your plan, which syncs through your own iCloud like your other accessories.

Homey

If you connect a Homey Pro, Home.io talks to it directly at the address you enter, on your home network. It reads your Homey's zones and devices (their names, zones and states, such as on or off, brightness, temperature, locked, open or closed, and the alarm's state) and sends your commands to them, an unlock or turning off the alarm only after your PIN, Face ID or Touch ID. All of this goes only between your device and your Homey, never through us or the home hub.

The API key you enter is kept only in that device's keychain: it is never synced to your other devices or put in Home.io's backups. The Homey's address and the list of your devices stay on that device too, and aren't in Home.io's backups, though your device's own iCloud or computer backup can include them. If you put a Homey device on your floor plan, hide it or change its icon, its name and room go with your plan, which syncs through your own iCloud like your other accessories.

Floor Plan on the Web

If you turn on Floor Plan on the Web, the device that's your home hub shows your floor plan as a web page that other devices on your home network can open: a picture of the plan with your rooms' names, the lights that are on, the doors and windows that are open and any alarm, plus the names of your floors. Anyone on your home network with the link can see it. The link holds a long random key, kept in that device's keychain; New Link makes the old one stop working. The page is view only and can't change anything. It's served by your device itself, only to devices on your home network and only while Home.io is open there; nothing about it goes through us or iCloud.

Your speakers

Apple Home doesn't share HomePods and other AirPlay speakers with apps, so to show them Home.io asks the Wi-Fi network your device is on which AirPlay speakers are there. It starts after you first open Music (or right away if you use Home Assistant or UniFi), and that first search is when iOS asks you for Local Network access. Home.io uses only each speaker's name and model, keeps the list in memory without saving it, and doesn't connect to your speakers or send them commands: tapping one opens Music, and you choose where the music plays with Apple's AirPlay. If you place a speaker on your floor plan, hide it or pick its icon, its name and room are saved with your plans like your other accessories.

Floor plans, settings and the home hub

Your floor plans, furniture, views and settings are saved on your device and synced between your own devices with Apple's iCloud, in your private iCloud storage. If you turn on the home hub, one of your devices at home (an iPad, Mac, iPhone or Apple TV) runs your workflows and shares your Home Assistant accessories, their states and what your workflows have done with your other devices through the same private iCloud storage, so you can control them away from home. If you turn on Home Alerts, the home hub puts a short note about what happened, such as a doorbell ring or your alarm going off with the accessory's name and room, in that storage so your other devices can notify you, and clears each note after a day. The PIN Home.io asks for before it unlocks a door, turns off your alarm or opens the garage is kept in that device's keychain, and each iPhone or iPad sets its own. If you use Home.io on an Apple TV, your iPhone or iPad puts a hash of the PIN (never the PIN itself) in that storage so the TV can check the code you enter. Apple holds this data for you under Apple's privacy policy; we cannot read it.

Readings, backups and the camera on a wall iPad

Home.io keeps your accessories' temperature, humidity and battery readings on your device for its History charts (the last 31 days). Backups you make, and the weekly automatic ones, hold your plans, settings and your Apple Home setup, and are saved where you choose or in your own iCloud Drive. If you turn on Wake When Someone Walks Up or Sleep When No One's There, the iPad's front camera looks for a face on the device itself: nothing is recorded, kept or sent anywhere, and a green dot shows while it's watching. Tell Home Assistant When Someone's There sends only "someone there" or "no one there" to your own Home Assistant server. A LiDAR scan keeps a report of how it went, with the shapes and sizes of your rooms but no photos, on your device only and out of backups; it's sent only if you tap Send Scan Report and choose where it goes.

AI

Home.io does not send anything to an AI service. The "Make a 3D Model with AI" prompt is text for you to copy into an assistant of your choosing; what you share there is between you and that service.

Location, weather and your yard

If you allow it, Home.io uses your location or your home address to show the real sun position, weather, weather alerts and recent snowfall on your model. For a home in the United States these come from the National Weather Service (api.weather.gov), which is sent your home's approximate position (to about a kilometre) while Home.io is open, about every 10 to 15 minutes. For a home anywhere else they come from Apple Weather. When you ask it to landscape your yard from an aerial photo, it sends the coordinates of your home to public map services to fetch the images and outlines for that spot: for the aerial photo, Microsoft Planetary Computer in the United States, basemap.at in Austria, PDOK in the Netherlands and swisstopo in Switzerland; and for the outline of the buildings, OpenStreetMap's Overpass service. These requests are made only when you use those features and carry no information about you besides the place.

Microphone and speech

Voice control listens only while you hold or tap the microphone button. Speech is turned into text by Apple's speech recognition to understand the command; Home.io does not record or keep your voice.

Purchases

Home.io Pro is a one-time purchase handled by the App Store. We receive no payment details.

No ads, no tracking

Home.io has no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party code that collects data. We do not sell or share information about you, because we do not have any.

Children

Home.io is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If Home.io ever changes so that it collects data, this page will say so first, and the App Store privacy information will be updated before that version is released.
